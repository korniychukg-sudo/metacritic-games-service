Eurogamer
These worlds don't just look beautiful, from Jakku's dunes and wrecked Star Destroyers you must thread between to Crait's white crystalline surface with red dust billowing underneath. They're also carved up into fascinating tracks that blend tight underground corridors where cornering is a constant discussion with death, and daringly wide-open outside sections where I once got so turned around I ended up going backwards. These are places that constantly shift in the way that they challenge you, and I suspect that better racers than me will spend an awfully long time working out the optimal strategies for handling each one.

MeuPlayStation
Star Wars: Galactic Racer combines precise arcade handling, distinct vehicle classes, and meaningful customization with a campaign built around risk, repetition, and gradual mastery. Track hazards, aggressive collisions, and the Ramjet system keep races tense, while the different vehicle types significantly change how each course is approached. The roguelite structure can become punishing, especially when unclear track design or abrupt difficulty spikes end a long run, and the lack of split-screen is a notable omission. Even so, its speed, mechanical depth, and strong audiovisual presentation give it a distinct identity among Star Wars racing games.

MagMachina
Oh how i wish i would love this game more. First the most important thing, SPEED and FEEL: People say it's fast, but i have to disagree. F-Zero, Extreme G, Episode 1 Racer - those games are fast! In Episode 1 Racer for example, you could really see how much scenery you were leaving behind at high speed. This game, on the other hand, only gives you the impression of speed, while the tracks themselves feel small. Then there’s the handling: the racers feel floaty, sloppy and soft to control. I miss the highly precise, sharp-edged handling of the really good old-school sci-fi racing games. Then there are the absurd DESIGN DECISIONS: no split-screen multiplayer, no option to select individual tracks, no time trials, no maps, and the decision to turn the Pod Racers into a separate game within the game. And finally, the TRACKS themselves: there simply aren’t enough of them, and they aren’t particularly creative. Another comparison to Episode I: Racer — there were sandcrawlers on the track, Tusken Raiders shooting at you, traps, and anti-gravity tunnels. Why do the tracks in a Star Wars racing game have to be so boring and unimaginative? Another criticism is the CAMPAIGN. It’s certainly creative, but I don’t need constant story interruptions in an arcade racer. I want to race, not chat with mechanics and other characters. I thought a Star Wars Racer from a Burnout-Team would be my dream game (i loved the Burnout-games!). Now i hope for some patches and updates, because i wont play this game much longer...
